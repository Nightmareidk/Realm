# Scroll-animation survey for Realm's new landing page (2026-10)

Purpose: about 70 sites with outstanding scroll animation, read against Realm's chosen direction:
(1) "Phones in motion": the app on phones that turn in 3D on scroll while the screens change per section;
(2) "Profile canvas": a Steam/MySpace-style customizable profile (banner, pinned clip, now playing, Spaces, badges) whose theme swaps on scroll;
plus a holographic profile card, a "who's here now" party lobby, a great loading page and rich (not flat-black) backgrounds.
Premium, not cartoony, not retro, not generic SaaS.

Evidence labels:
- **verified visually**: scrolled in real Chrome with wheel events; 4x4 frame sheets in `scroll-captures/_<name>.jpg`, tech in `scroll-captures/_report.json`.
- **from source/text**: from the site's own text or HTML, award write-ups, case studies, Codrops articles, press or forum posts. Not seen moving by us.

Technique shorthand: GSAP/ST = GSAP + ScrollTrigger; R3F = React Three Fiber; seq = image-sequence scrubbing on canvas; CSS-SDA = CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()/view()`).

## 6. Synthesis

Count: 18 verified visually, 14 product/brand pages, 19 agency and award sites, 12 social apps, 7 AI-built entries. That is 70 rows, about 68 distinct sites (Vercel appears twice; Gas is defunct). The 18 frame sheets are the only motion we saw ourselves; everything else is from source or text.

### 6.1 Fifteen patterns that feel premium

1. **One hero object that persists and turns across the page.** Lando Norris's helmet, Dogstudio's dog, Arc's browser window, Apple's buds. Continuity reads as craft.
2. **Same object, new skin.** The theme swaps while the content stays (Arc windows, Partiful's invite card, Dogstudio's background). This *is* Realm's Profile canvas.
3. **Pinned stage with cycling content.** Lenis ("Why smooth scroll?" plus 4 reasons) and Apple sticky stages. The scroll advances a story while the frame holds still.
4. **Motion that resolves to a composed still.** Apple's start-frame/end-frame films: every scroll position is a good poster, so nothing looks mid-glitch when you stop.
5. **Physical weight.** Inertia, damping and springs (Oryzo's coaster, Vercel's lanyard badge, Lenis), never linear tweens.
6. **Colour as reward.** Mostly ink, then colour blooms at key beats (Ponpon Mania's dream, Unseen's two-colour discipline, Discord's coloured pods).
7. **Layered dark grounds, not black and not noise.** A low-frequency gradient (Stripe, Lenis horizon), a glow bleeding from *under* the UI (Raycast), a static grain layer, and sparse stars. Active Theory's teal-to-violet nebula is the ceiling.
8. **Real product and real content as the art.** Linear, Discord and Threads' ring of real posts. Live UI beats illustration.
9. **Iridescence used once and physically.** The AirPods halo, the Pokémon-CSS holofoil that responds to angle, Apple Card's single glint.
10. **Windows that open instead of fades.** Clip-path or mask reveals and warped planes that settle (Zentry, Lusion's reel).
11. **Annotated 3D.** HUD callouts pinned to objects (Igloo). This is great for badges and profile stats.
12. **A loader that is part of the story.** SVG-first then shader (Vercel Ship), an emblem assembling (Active Theory, Igloo), and an exit that becomes the first frame (the Rivian R2 wordmark expanding to the edges).
13. **Restraint.** One signature moment per section and calm sections between (Teenage Engineering, Linear, Apple).
14. **Visible presence.** Other people shown as beings in a space (Messenger's live messengers, Bruno's "whispers", Discord's member list).
15. **Light 3D, budgeted.** Messenger's 5.7 MB first load, OGL instead of three.js (Ponpon), CSS-3D card rings (Codrops), and scrubbed video instead of 200 PNGs (LearnWithHasan: 4.7x lighter, 5x faster to draw).

### 6.2 Ten patterns that feel cheap or AI-made

1. **The same fade-up on every section,** plus bounce easing and count-up stats (the top motion tell in `avoid-ai-design`).
2. **Purple or indigo nebula glow everywhere.** This is exactly what got Realm's last look called "AI slop".
3. **Cute floating mascots, stickers and candy 3D blobs on a perspective grid** (the Spline-hero cliché, Discord's crowd, our own astronauts).
4. **Inter plus gradient-filled headline text** (`bg-clip-text`) plus weightless copy ("Build faster. Ship smarter.").
5. **Three rounded glass cards in a row** with line icons, and reflexive glassmorphism on everything.
6. **AI-video "explosion" flipbooks** with background seams and 1080p softness. This is the 2026 "Apple scroll in an afternoon" template (Scroll-Cinematic, Chase AI).
7. **The default three.js "camera pushes through floating objects" scene,** and particles that chase the cursor for no reason.
8. **Text scramble or SplitText kinetic type on every heading.** It is great once (Obys, Active Theory) and template-like when repeated.
9. **The "tasteful" second-order defaults:** cream plus terracotta, near-black plus one acid-green accent, mono all-caps labels, 01/02/03 numbering, fake window-chrome dots.
10. **Scroll-jacking with no progress cue,** content hidden below the fold, ignoring `prefers-reduced-motion`, and consent banners covering the hero (our Nothing capture).

### 6.4 Concrete ideas for Realm

Hard constraints (from `realm-performance-lessons`): the owner's Radeon 610M iGPU. No full-screen noise shaders; soft layers render at half resolution; everything goes through `warmScene` before drawing; pause off-screen; adaptive DPR; a static composite under `prefers-reduced-motion` and Pause motion.

#### Loading page: "Tuning in"

1. **0 ms:** HTML/CSS only. The Eclipse mark (a dark disc with its aurora rim) on a midnight-blue ground (not #000) with fine grain, so first paint is instant (Vercel Ship's SVG-first rule).
2. **Progress = the rim.** The aurora rim draws round the eclipse as *real* progress comes in (assets, plus `holdWarmup` finishing shader compiles), so the 0.5 s PMREM stall hides behind it.
3. **A quiet live line under it,** honest per Realm's copy rules. Pre-launch: "Spaces are opening". Post-launch: a real "1,204 people hanging out right now", rendered as a small dot-matrix counter (Nothing's Glyph idea, not a cartoon).
4. **Exit:** the rim expands to the viewport edges (Rivian's R2 move) and becomes the bezel of the centre phone. The loader *is* the first frame of the hero, with no cut.
5. **Rules:** skip it on repeat visits in the same session, hold for at least 600 ms and at most about 2.5 s, and keep it keyboard-skippable.

#### Section by section

1. **Hero, "Phones in motion".**
   - Stage: three phones on a pinned stage, the centre one forward and two flanking at depth. They sit on a low-frequency gradient with a glow pooling under them (Raycast).
   - Motion: scroll turns them like Lando's helmet, with spring damping, never linear. Each phone resolves to a composed still at every section boundary (Apple).
   - Screens: Rive or looping video textures (Spaces chat, a clip, a profile).
   - Build: R3F with one GLB (Meshy is approved, textures capped at 512 except the screen). A scrubbed-video fallback for low-end GPUs (dense keyframes, about 1 MB).
2. **Spaces (group chat).**
   - The centre phone scales up in perspective until its screen fills the viewport (Raycast/Linear). The Spaces UI comes alive: rooms as tabs, bubbles arriving in step with scroll.
   - A small presence rail shows avatars with "in voice", "watching" and "playing" states.
3. **Feed (posts + clips).**
   - Real-looking posts and clips fly in from a tilted 3D card cloud and settle into the feed grid (Framer), or peel off a stack (Codrops 3D Stack).
   - The **pinned clip** opens through a clip-path window that warps and settles (Zentry + Lusion).
   - CSS 3D, not WebGL.
4. **Profile canvas: the signature.**
   - A full Steam/MySpace-style profile pins to the screen: banner, pinned clip, now playing, Spaces, badges.
   - Scroll swaps **4–5 themes** (for example Midnight, Arcade neon, Film grain, Garden, Chrome). Each swap wipes in the scroll direction. Fonts, frames, banner and accent change, and **the page background re-tints to match** (Arc + Dogstudio).
   - A Partiful-style tab strip (Banner, Font, Frame, Effects) highlights what is changing, so the customization *reads*.
   - Colour is the reward: the page is ink-dark until this section (Ponpon).
   - **Now playing** is the most alive tile: album art, a live waveform and a friend reacting (Airbuds).
   - Badges get Igloo-style HUD callouts on hover.
5. **Holographic card, as a profile element and not the identity.**
   - The profile's ID card lifts off the canvas onto a lanyard (the Vercel badge recipe).
   - Foil from Pokémon-CSS layers: tilt to the cursor on desktop, gyro on phones, and a scroll-driven glare sweep.
   - **Exactly one** iridescent moment on the page (the AirPods halo). It must not become a "trading card site".
6. **"Who's here now" party lobby.**
   - A small dark stage (or a tiny planet, as in Messenger) where friends' avatars drift in. Presence chips ("in Movie Night", "playing", "listening").
   - "Whispers" of recent joins (Bruno), real data once live.
   - A kinetic headline here only.
7. **Boards** (a short beat). A calm editorial strip of real threads. Teenage Engineering-style restraint gives the eye a rest before the finale.
8. **Close.**
   - The three phones return and settle face-up into the final still.
   - A witty, honest end line (Discord's "You can't scroll anymore. Better go chat." is the bar, not the copy).
   - A giant Realm wordmark footer on the gradient ground.

**Background system, page-wide.** Midnight base; one half-resolution mesh gradient; a static grain plus stars layer; per-section accent glow from beneath the focal object; tints driven by the active profile theme (CSS vars written only when they change). No purple nebula, no mascots, no full-screen noise.

# Ship the chat baseline, then own presence

Realm's phone app should not try to out-feature WhatsApp, Telegram or Discord, because by late 2026 the messaging feature set has converged almost completely; it should ship that shared baseline cleanly and quickly, then win the two surfaces nobody has solved on a phone: **live presence ("here now") and voice rooms**. The recommended order is three waves. First comes a stable five-destination shell and the full messaging loop (inbox, a chat screen with replies, reactions, pins, Seen, typing, edits, images and push) on an offline-first data layer. Second comes one-tap voice rooms with a persistent call bar, a "here now" strip on Home, and granular presence privacy. Third comes the identity and friend-group layer (the person's theme colour carried everywhere, polls and ready checks, events with RSVP, role tags), with the feed after that. The navigation rule comes from Discord, which reshaped its mobile navigation **three times between December 2023 and June 2026** and whose CTO conceded that the mobile-first rethink did not work for people who use both desktop and mobile: mirror the web app's destinations, keep Spaces and Messages one gesture apart, and never hide a switcher behind a long-press alone. The technical forcing function is immediate: **remote push has been unavailable in Expo Go on Android since SDK 53**, LiveKit and MMKV need native code, and **Expo shipped SDK 58 on 15 October 2026 with a store Expo Go that will open only SDK 58 projects within weeks**, so Realm should move to an EAS development build now. Android 16 makes edge-to-edge mandatory and keyboard handling app-driven, and Google Play's API 36 floor, account-deletion, user-generated-content and child-safety rules apply to a social app from its first upload. The anti-patterns are equally clear from 2025-26 backlash: surfaces users cannot turn off, forced navigation migrations, presence leaks, ringing group calls, glass and blur everywhere, paywalled identity, and security claims the product cannot back.

## Three Discord redesigns in 30 months set the navigation rules

Discord is the closest analogue to Realm, and its mobile history is a controlled experiment in what friend groups and gamers will tolerate. In **December 2023** Discord replaced its sliding panels with four bottom tabs (Servers, Messages, Notifications, You), arguing the old app had become a cramped copy of desktop, and claimed app open time fell **55% on Android** and 43% on iOS ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/improving-our-mobile-experience)). Users hated it anyway: launch-day threads complained of extra taps, DMs that were harder to reach, a bottom bar that wasted space, and the loss of the swipe that opened the member list, with Android users reporting the most friction ([ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/discord-mobile-has-put-out-a-terrible-update-with-worse-ui.792552/)). By **May 2024** Discord merged Servers and Messages back into one Home, saying the split made it hard to switch mentally between mobile and desktop and that the confusion when servers and friends were separated was loud and clear ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/refining-discords-mobile-experience-with-your-feedback)). In **June 2026** it replaced the tabs entirely with the "You Bar", a single server-centric system in which a bell holds notifications and a swipe across the bar flips between DMs and the current server, justified by usage data showing most time was spent navigating servers and channels ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/you-bar)). It cannot be disabled ([Friendcord](https://friendcordapp.com/blog/discord-mobile-ui-squarer-you-bar-2026)). In March 2026 Discord's CTO said the mobile-first rethink had not worked for most people who use both platforms, that the design system is being unified across desktop and mobile, and that the team wants fewer buttons in the chat bar ([PiunikaWeb](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/03/19/discord-mobile-app-redesign-desktop-ui/)).

Instagram ran the opposite play. In October 2025 Mosseri reorganised the bar around actual use (Feed first, Reels second, DMs third), made every tab horizontally swipeable, and shipped it **opt-in first** ([Mosseri on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DPpGG_IiLgm); [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/10/13/instagram-tests-reels-first-redesign-with-dms-at-the-center/)). Slack's 2020 redesign made the complementary point that every swipe shortcut needs a visible on-screen button ([Slack](https://slack.com/blog/productivity/simpler-more-organized-slack-mobile-app)), and Discord's opt-in Launchpad, a mobile equivalent of a command palette for jumping to any server, DM or channel, shows that heavy users need a fast switcher on top of the main navigation ([Discord on X](https://x.com/discord/status/1879268347478773819)).

Four durable rules fall out of this. Realm's phone app should **use the same destinations as the web app**, because the cross-platform mental model is exactly what Discord broke. Spaces and Messages should sit **one tap or one swipe apart**, never in a hierarchy where one is buried. Every gesture shortcut needs a visible equivalent, and the Space switcher must never be reachable only by long-press. And because Realm is new, it carries no migration debt; the expensive mistake would be shipping a provisional navigation and forcing a redesign on users later. A five-destination bar (Home, Spaces, Messages, Inbox, and You shown as the person's avatar) fits Material's three-to-five-destination guidance ([Material Components](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/BottomNavigation.md)); WhatsApp on iPhone likewise puts the active account's picture in its tab bar ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/whatsapp-new-features-simplify-storage-switch-accounts/)). This exact set is a design hypothesis, not something the sources prove, and it should be reconciled with whatever five destinations the web app already uses. Borrowing Discord's swipe-on-the-bar to flip between the last Space and the last DM is a good shortcut precisely because it lives on the bar, away from swipe-to-reply in the content and the system back gesture at the screen edges. The web's quick switcher should become a mobile Launchpad-style search reachable from a visible button.

### Home should answer "who is here" before "what is new"

Every strong precedent puts people and live activity at the top. Discord's DM home shows friends' current activity (in voice, playing a game, listening to music) above conversations ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/improving-our-mobile-experience)), and kept that "Active Now" strip after the 2024 merge so friends can start a hangout ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2024/05/31/discord-redesign-for-a-game)). Slack's Home sorts channels and DMs that need attention first ([Slack](https://slack.com/blog/productivity/simpler-more-organized-slack-mobile-app)), and Instagram still opens on Stories above Feed ([Mosseri](https://www.threads.com/@mosseri/post/DPpGG_IiLgm)). Realm's Home should therefore stack a **"here now" strip** (avatars of friends and Space members currently in voice rooms or playing, styled like a stories row, each one tap from joining), then a **needs-attention list** (unread DMs, mentions and replies across Spaces), then Spaces with live counts, with the feed arriving beneath these later. Suggested or public Spaces belong in a separate discovery surface, as Threads did with its Communities hub ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/threads-adds-new-personalization-and-community-features-as-it-reaches-500m-monthly-users/)), not mixed into a friend-group home.

### A Space screen is a header, rooms, and people who are visibly inside them

On phones every successful community app still uses a vertical list of rooms per community, with members, media, links and pins behind the header ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/improving-our-mobile-experience)). The member list was Discord's most persistent mobile complaint after the swipe that opened it was repurposed for reply ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/refining-discords-mobile-experience-with-your-feedback)). Geneva's typed rooms (chat, forum, audio, broadcast) plus one events calendar across all groups showed how well typed rooms map onto friend groups ([Geneva](https://genevachat.com/about)). Realm's Space screen should carry the Space icon, name and a live "here now" count in the header (tap for details, members, pins and events), then text rooms and voice rooms as separate sections, with **voice-room rows showing occupant avatars inline**. Discord's August 2026 refresh codified the rule "people are circles, things are squircles" and moved rarely used actions (threads, apps) behind a "+" in the composer while frequent ones moved right, nearer the thumb ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/improving-mobile-with-squircles-styles-and-spacing)). Realm can apply the same distinction in its own shape language (round people, Spaces in its pill family) without adopting Discord's look.

### First run should end inside a friend's Space, not an empty Home

Friend-finding in social apps runs on invite links, codes, QR and opt-in contact matching. Discord's contact matching uploads hashed numbers and matches only when both people opt in ([Discord](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/360061878534)); the walkie-talkie app ten ten adds friends by PIN only, which makes it private by default ([Vodafone](https://www.vodafone.co.uk/newscentre/smart-living/digital-parenting/digital-parenting-pro/ten-ten/)). Two shutdowns sharpen the lesson: Guilded closed on 19 December 2025 ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/11/guilded-to-shut-down-on-december-19-2025-as-focus-shifts-to-roblox-communities/)) and Geneva folded into Bumble BFF, whose migration kept members' groups and messages intact ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/18/bumble-bffs-revamped-app-is-here-focusing-on-friend-groups-and-community-building)), while Root fights the empty-community problem by importing Discord server templates ([Netto's Game Room](https://www.nettosgameroom.com/2026/02/discord-alternative-root-allows-users.html?m=1)). Features do not retain friend groups; the network does. Realm's existing identity-first sign-up (the profile card and colour pick) is the right opening, and it should be followed by "join your friends" (paste a link, scan a QR, enter a code, or opt into contact matching with a one-line explanation of what leaves the phone), landing in the first Space. An invite link opened before install should still land in that Space after install. An empty Home should be an invite action and a "start a Space" path, not an illustration.

Permissions follow the same logic. Android 13+ ships notifications off for new installs and tells developers not to prompt at first launch ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/notification-permission)). Permission-priming guidance warns that a prompt shown before people understand the app gets a reflexive no that is hard to win back, and recommends an in-app primer before the one-shot system dialog ([Superwall](https://superwall.com/solutions/optimize-permission-requests-ask-at-the-right-time-and-you-shall-receive); [Appcues](https://www.appcues.com/blog/mobile-permission-priming)). Realm should ask for notifications when the first friend joins or the first message arrives, and for the microphone on the first "Join voice" tap.

## The chat screen is a solved anatomy, so execution is the product

WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage, Signal, Messenger and Snapchat share one chat skeleton: a header with name and presence, timestamped bubbles grouped by sender, a per-message delivery state, an in-thread typing indicator, quoted replies, reactions attached to the bubble, pinned messages in a banner, and a composer whose microphone becomes Send. WhatsApp moved its typing indicator from the header into the thread in December 2024 as an animated bubble that shows the faces of everyone typing in a group ([ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/!technology/whatsapp-rolls-out-typing-indicators-feature-to-make-group-chat-more-engaging-enn24120604175)); iMessage only gained group typing indicators with iOS 26 ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-elevates-the-iphone-experience-with-ios-26/)). Pins converged on **three per chat**: WhatsApp offers 24-hour, 7-day and 30-day pins cycled from a banner ([Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/whatsapp-three-pinned-messages-3427583)), and Signal's January 2026 pins add a "forever" duration and let group admins decide who may pin ([Signal](https://signal.org/blog/pinned-messages/)).

### Seen should follow Telegram's multi-device model

Receipt semantics are the main design fork. WhatsApp shows grey ticks for sent and delivered and blue for read, turns a group message blue only when every member has read it, makes receipts reciprocal, and gives per-person times under Message Info ([WhatsApp Help](https://faq.whatsapp.com/665923838265756/?locale=af_ZA&cms_platform=android)). Telegram deliberately has **no "delivered" state**, because one account lives on many devices; one tick means the message reached the cloud and two mean it was opened ([Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq)). Signal shows Sending, Sent, Delivered and Read, with Read visible only when both people allow it ([Signal Support](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360007320751-How-do-I-know-if-my-message-was-delivered-or-read)). Realm is multi-device by design (web plus phone) and stores messages on its own servers, so Telegram's model fits it best: **Sending, Sent, Seen, and a visible Failed state with tap-to-retry**. In DMs, "Seen" sits under the last message you sent; in group chats, a small row of avatars marks how far each person has read, with a "Seen by" sheet from the long-press menu. The avatar style is attributed to Messenger only by low-quality sources, so treat it as a pattern to test rather than a documented standard. Realm's web receipts are already mutual, matching the reciprocal toggle WhatsApp and Signal use.

### Long-press, swipe and double-tap are the universal gesture set

Long-press is the universal entry point: it opens a reaction row above the bubble and an action menu below. Swipe-to-reply is the near-universal shortcut. Discord added it in 2023 ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/improving-our-mobile-experience)), users describe long-press-then-Reply as too cumbersome for frequent use ([Discourse Meta](https://meta.discourse.org/t/swiping-to-initiate-a-reply-in-chat/238212?tl=en)), and chat SDKs ship swipe actions as a default override point ([Stream](https://getstream.io/chat/docs/sdk/react-native/v9/contexts/messages-context)). Double-tap sends a quick reaction: Telegram's default thumbs-up is configurable ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/blog/reactions-spoilers-translations)), and WhatsApp tested a heart and then the full tray ([WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-beta-for-android-2-24-24-24-whats-new/)). Timing matters: a developer cloning iMessage's quick-reaction bar found their 0.25-second long-press felt wrong against iMessage's apparent 0.5 to 1.0 seconds ([Apple Developer Forums](https://origin-devforums.apple.com/forums/profile/EdifyCoder7)), and long-press is more idiomatic on Android than on iOS ([UX Magazine](https://uxmag.com/articles/using-interactions-to-shape-user-behavior-patterns)). Edit and delete windows have converged too: WhatsApp and Messenger allow **15-minute edits** with an "edited" label ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/how-to-edit-messages-in-whatsapp)), iMessage allows five edits in 15 minutes and unsend within 2 minutes ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/messages/ichtd68328c6)), and Signal allows ten edits and delete-for-everyone within 24 hours ([Signal](https://signal.org/blog/new-features-fall-2023/); [Signal Support](https://support.signal.org/hc/en-us/articles/360050426432-Delete-for-everyone)). Realm should adopt a 15-minute edit window with an "edited" label, delete-for-everyone for 24 hours leaving a placeholder, and a double-tap that sends the person's own chosen quick reaction drawn from the web's quick six.

No vendor documents bubble-grouping thresholds, unread dividers or "jump to latest" behaviour, so these must come from hands-on study rather than citation. The observable convention is a "new messages" divider at the first unread, a floating jump-to-latest pill with a count, and older history loading automatically as the user scrolls up.

### The inbox is filter chips, requests and a sacred DM list

The chat list is reverse-chronological with pins, mute, archive and unread counts, and since 2024 it has gained a filter-chip bar. WhatsApp added All, Unread, Groups and Favorites in April 2024 and custom Lists in October 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/31/whatsapp-users-can-now-filter-chats-with-customized-lists)); Telegram uses folders. Stranger friction is now standard: iOS 26 silences unknown senders in a separate folder ([Apple](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2025/06/apple-elevates-the-iphone-experience-with-ios-26/)), and WhatsApp shows a safety screen when a non-contact adds you to a group, with notifications silenced until you choose to stay ([BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/08/06/whatsapp-launches-new-tools-to-stop-scammers/)). For Realm, a chip per squad (user-defined lists of DMs and groups) is a cheap, familiar way to organise many group chats, and message requests from non-friends belong in a silenced bucket.

### Notifications have converged on "only what is addressed to me"

In April 2025 WhatsApp added a group "Highlights" mode that notifies only for mentions, replies and saved contacts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/10/whatsapp-introduces-new-features-across-chats-calls-and-channels)); Telegram's muted groups still alert on mentions and replies ([Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq)). WhatsApp's August 2026 "@all" pierces mute, but **only admins can use it in groups over 32 members**, and each person can mute it ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/04/whatsapp-adds-all-mentions-and-poll-upgrades/)). Discord's per-server levels are All, Only mentions or Nothing, with separate suppression of everyone-pings ([Discord Support](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/215253258)), and it kept a dedicated notifications bell even in the tabless You Bar ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/you-bar)). Realm's Inbox tab should split "For you" (mentions, replies, DMs) from "Activity" (reactions, joins, events); small friend Spaces should default to All and large ones to mentions only; and an everyone-ping should be admin-only above a size threshold and mutable.

On Android, chat notifications must be **conversation notifications**: MessagingStyle, linked to a long-lived sharing shortcut with person data, which earns the conversation section at the top of the shade, a "priority" option, bubbles and per-conversation sounds ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/conversations)). Direct reply needs a mutable intent and a unique request code per conversation, or a reply can land in the wrong chat, and the notification should be updated rather than cancelled after replying ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/notifications/build-notification)). Android 16 also reportedly lowers alert volume for rapid bursts and force-groups an app's notifications ([Android Central](https://androidcentral.com/apps-software/android-16-has-a-new-trick-to-put-a-damper-on-annoying-app-pings); [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/android-16-force-group-notifications-3565400)), which argues for summarising busy group chats rather than posting one alert per message.

## Voice and presence are the surfaces nobody has solved on a phone

Mobile voice is the least-finished area in the category, and that is Realm's opening. Discord's mobile app still opens a pre-join sheet when you tap a voice channel ([Discord patch notes](https://discord.com/blog/discord-patch-notes-september-8-2026)), and its own roadmap promises desktop-style joining and leaving on mobile ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/you-bar)). Users complain that the Android "return to call" indicator is tiny ([Discord Support community](https://support.discord.com/hc/de/community/posts/360050806714-Call-button-in-android)) and ask for auto-mute when a phone call arrives, which Discord does not offer ([Discord Support community](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/1500000513021-Mobile-Discord-Incoming-Phone-Call-Issue)). In 2026 Discord was still fixing a push-to-talk setting that reset on every restart and Android picture-in-picture bugs ([Discord patch notes](https://discord.com/blog/discord-patch-notes-september-8-2026)), and it added a confirmation step after accidental call-button presses ([Discord patch notes](https://discord.com/blog/discord-patch-notes-june-4-2026)). WhatsApp's 2024 group voice chats show the cleaner pattern: they **start quietly with a notification instead of ringing**, show a banner of who has joined, keep mute and leave controls docked at the top of the chat while you keep texting, and end automatically when empty ([Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/amp/whatsapp-makes-discord-like-voice-chats-official-lets-you-hop-in-and-out/)). Slack huddles start muted when you are alone, and a "Join Here" control moves a desktop huddle to the phone ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/742357/what-is-a-slack-huddle-and-how-do-i-start-one/)).

Realm's voice design should therefore be one tap from a voice-room row to joined, with a first-time microphone primer and a long-press preview of who is inside and the room's text, which Discord already lets users read without joining ([Design Compass](https://designcompass.org/en/2024/05/31/discord-redesign-for-a-game)). The in-call screen should use tiles lit by each person's theme colour. Discord gives themed call tiles only to Nitro subscribers ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/improving-mobile-with-squircles-styles-and-spacing)), so offering them to everyone is a visible way to be slightly ahead. Swiping the call screen down should collapse it into a **persistent call bar docked above the tab bar**, showing who is speaking with large mute, deafen and leave targets, mirrored by an Android foreground-service notification with mute and leave actions. Output routing between speaker, earpiece and Bluetooth belongs one tap away, settings such as push-to-talk must persist, and the app should mute itself when a phone call takes audio focus. A "continue on phone" handoff from a web voice session matches Slack's "Join Here". Two products point to a later differentiator: ten ten's walkie-talkie, which plays a friend's voice instantly even on a locked phone ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2787985)), and Clubhouse's 2023 pivot from public live rooms to friend-centric voice chats ([Engadget](https://engadget.com/clubhouse-is-pivoting-from-live-audio-to-group-messaging-001520371.html)). Both suggest that friend-scale voice beats stage-scale voice, and that a hold-to-talk "ping" to a friend or room could sit beside full voice rooms.

Presence is the social glue that makes voice discoverable, and also the fastest way to lose trust. Discord's long-running forum complaints say Invisible does not reliably hide people from Active Now and cannot be set per server or per person; these are forum posts dating from about 2020, not verified behaviour ([Discord Support community](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360058196572--Active-Now-Serious-Privacy-Concerns)). Instagram's opt-in friend Map drew stalking fears in August 2025 from users who believed they had been placed on it without agreeing ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/social-media/instagrams-new-map-feature-triggers-safety-fears-meta-blames-misunderstanding-78562.htm)). Realm's "here now" should be granular from day one: a global status, per-Space visibility, per-activity toggles for voice, game and music presence, and **a live preview line showing exactly what friends see**, which is the single detail that would have defused Instagram's confusion. Presence should appear where it leads to action (voice-room rows, the Home strip, the You tab) rather than as a passive online list. Realm's existing location decision, one-time place cards and never live tracking, already matches this lesson.

## Users forgive features they can ignore and punish ones they cannot

The 2025-26 backlash has one shape. WhatsApp's April 2025 Meta AI button, which could not be removed, drew complaints focused on the missing off switch and interest from EU lawmakers ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/websites-apps/whatsapp-users-fume-over-new-meta-ai-button-that-you-cant-remove-heres-what-it-does); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/05/11/metas-new-optional-ai-chatbot-in-apps-like-whatsapp-is-proving-controversial-heres-why)). WhatsApp then confined ads to the Updates tab, which its executive said users could simply ignore ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/06/16/whatsapp-status-ads/)). Snapchat's 5 GB cap on free Memories storage was attacked as a tax on people's own memories, with a petition of about 9,000 signatures ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/snapchat-introduces-paid-plans-for-memories-storage-faces-backlash-1.500293058)). Apple's Liquid Glass drew legibility complaints that led to an opacity toggle, and it was still divisive months after launch ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-26-is-still-proving-divisive-with-users-months-after-it-launched-and-i-think-i-know-why)). Discord's 2023 darker default produced complaints that led to contrast and saturation sliders ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/refining-discords-mobile-experience-with-your-feedback)). Praise, by contrast, goes to clear organisation (DMs in one place), speed, and cleaner layouts. Users tolerate polls, pins, Lists and a mutable @all; they revolt against things they cannot remove, charges on value they already had, and redesigns that bury DMs.

The feature baseline is now nearly identical everywhere, because the apps copied one another. iOS 26 gave Messages WhatsApp-style polls, backgrounds and group typing ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/phones/ios/apples-messages-app-is-getting-the-whatsapp-treatment-here-are-5-new-features-coming-to-ios-26)); Signal added polls in November 2025 ([Signal](https://signal.org/blog/polls/)) and member labels in March 2026 ([Signal blog](https://signal.org/blog/)); Telegram added member tags ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/blog/member-tags-disable-sharing-and-more)). The table separates what Realm must simply have from where it can lead.

| Area | 2026 baseline (who ships it) | Where Realm can lead |
|---|---|---|
| Receipts and typing | Per-message state, in-thread typing bubble with faces (WhatsApp, iMessage) | Sent then Seen for multi-device, read-position avatars in groups, reciprocal toggle |
| Replies, reactions, pins | Swipe to reply, long-press tray, double-tap reaction, three pins (WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal) | Personal quick reaction; Space-chosen reaction sets, as Telegram admins can choose ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/blog/reactions-spoilers-translations)) |
| Edits and deletes | 15-minute edit label, 24-hour delete-for-everyone (WhatsApp, Messenger, Signal) | Same windows, stated plainly in the menu |
| Inbox | Filter chips, custom Lists, pins, mute, archive, requests (WhatsApp, iOS 26) | Squad chips; DMs and Space rooms in one needs-attention list |
| Notifications | Highlights mode, admin-gated @all, Android conversation notifications | "Ping me when 2+ friends are in voice" as a quiet opt-in per Space |
| Group tools | Polls, events with RSVP, member tags, ownership transfer (Signal, WhatsApp, Telegram) | Ready checks, session polls with end times, role tags as in-game ranks |
| Voice | Pre-join sheet (Discord), docked controls (WhatsApp voice chats) | One-tap join, persistent call bar, themed tiles for everyone |
| Presence | Active Now with weak controls (Discord) | Granular controls with a "what friends see" preview |
| Identity | Paid cosmetics (Discord Nitro themes, nameplates, frames) | Theme colour across profile, You tab and call tiles, free |

The friend-group evidence is strong. Snap says **35% of US Snapchatters aged 13 to 24 use group chats to make plans**, which is why it shipped Plans with Going, Maybe and Can't Go replies in September 2026 ([Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/making-plans-irl)), and WhatsApp's April 2025 events added "Maybe" and "+1" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/04/10/whatsapp-introduces-new-features-across-chats-calls-and-channels)). Snapchat's group streaks prove engagement value but carry obvious pressure risk ([Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/infiniteretentionandgroupstreaks)). The market window is real as well: Discord's February 2026 age-verification rollout pushed users toward alternatives such as Stoat, whose servers buckled ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/computing/social-media/so-many-discord-users-are-flocking-to-this-alternative-platform-its-making-stoat-crash)), yet those alternatives are weakest on mobile voice and feature parity. An app with Discord's structure, Instagram's polish (swipeable tabs, identity-forward profiles, a stories-style live strip) and calmer, stable navigation has room to lead.

## Android 16 and WCAG 2.2 set the floor for a branded dark app

Edge-to-edge is no longer a choice. Since **31 August 2026, new apps and updates on Google Play must target Android 16 (API 36)**, with extensions to 1 November 2026 ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/requirements/target-sdk)), and apps targeting API 36 cannot opt out of edge-to-edge ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)). React Native 0.81 already targets API 36 and deprecated the legacy `SafeAreaView` in favour of `react-native-safe-area-context` ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/08/12/react-native-0.81)), and Expo SDK 55 removed the `edgeToEdgeEnabled` option and turned most status-bar and navigation-bar methods into no-ops ([Expo SDK 55](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-55)). Realm must pad headers with status-bar and cutout insets, pad the tab bar with the navigation-bar inset, let lists scroll behind transparent bars, and keep its own swipes (reply, carousels, sheet drags) clear of the system gesture zones, which take priority over the app ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/edge-to-edge)). Google explicitly discourages a translucent gesture navigation bar. Most importantly for chat, **`adjustResize` no longer resizes the window for the keyboard**; the app receives keyboard (IME) insets and must move the composer itself ([Margelo](https://margelo.com/blog/deep-dive-in-keyboard-handling)).

Back navigation needs deliberate rules. Apps targeting API 36 on Android 16 get predictive back by default, and the legacy back callback is no longer called ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)). React Native's `BackHandler` mostly keeps working ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/08/12/react-native-0.81)), but react-native-screens maintainers called fragment-level predictive back unlikely in v4 ([react-native-screens](https://github.com/software-mansion/react-native-screens/discussions/2540)), so Expo still defaults `android.predictiveBackGestureEnabled` to false ([Expo config](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/config/app/)) while SDK 56's experimental Stack v5 adds predictive back support ([Expo SDK 56](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-56)). Realm should keep Expo's default for now and keep back strictly hierarchical: back dismisses the keyboard, then any sheet or menu, then dialogs, then pushed screens; from another tab's root it returns to Home; from Home it exits. A `BackHandler` that returns true counts as consuming back, so it should be registered only while a sheet or modal is open ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/predictive-back-gesture)), and Realm should never add a "press back again to exit" prompt.

Large screens are no longer optional either. On displays at least 600dp wide, Android 16 ignores orientation and resizability locks for apps targeting API 36 ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)), and Android 17 removes the temporary opt-out ([Android Developers blog](https://developer.android.com/blog/posts/prepare-your-app-for-the-resizability-and-orientation-changes-in-android-17)). Realm should plan a bottom bar below 600dp, a side rail at 600 to 840dp, and a conversation list beside the open thread above 840dp. These are the compact, medium and expanded window size classes, which change at runtime with rotation and folding ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/layouts/adaptive/use-window-size-classes)). Material 3 Expressive shortened the navigation bar from 80dp to 64dp ([Material Components](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/BottomNavigation.md)), and Google reports, from its own 46 studies with more than 18,000 participants, that its larger targets and stronger containment helped users over 45 perform on par with younger ones ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/05/28/google-ushers-in-age-of-expressive-interfaces-with-material-design-update/amp/)). A branded app should borrow those accessibility traits and Android's interaction conventions, not Material's look: feedback with an action such as "Message deleted, Undo" belongs in a snackbar-style message rather than a toast, which Android 12+ limits to two lines stamped with the app icon ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/notifiers/toasts)), and sheets should dismiss with back and carry unique pane titles that TalkBack announces ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles)). Touch targets should be at least **48 by 48dp** ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)), double WCAG 2.2's 24-pixel AA minimum ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)). Haptics should be sparse; Google's own rule is that no haptics beats buzzy haptics ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/haptics/haptics-principles)).

Dark-only is acceptable, but it has to be declared. Expo's `android.userInterfaceStyle` **defaults to light** and requires `expo-system-ui` ([Expo config](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/config/app/)), so an unconfigured Realm build risks a white flash at launch and dark-on-dark system bar icons. Android does not require branded apps to adopt dynamic colour; it offers keeping brand colours fixed or "harmonizing" them, which shifts their tone for balance and accessible contrast ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/theming/dynamic-colors)). That same idea is the key to Realm's look. Because each person's theme colour lights the ground, every text and surface token derived from it must pass **4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for icons, borders and unread or presence dots** ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)). The check belongs at the moment the colour is picked, with the tone adjusted automatically, not left to luck. Discord solves the same problem by placing profile content in outlined boxes on themed backgrounds ([n3tc0rd](https://n3tc0rd.digitalpress.blog/discord-profile-updates/)). Online, unread and Seen states also need a shape or label as well as colour, because WCAG 1.4.1 forbids conveying meaning through colour alone.

For TalkBack, each message bubble should be one focusable element whose label carries sender, time, text and state ("Aisha, 2:14 PM: See you at eight. Seen."). Reply, react, copy and delete must be exposed as accessibility actions, because swipes and long-presses alone are invisible to screen-reader users, and the generic long-press announcement should be relabelled ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/views/principles-views)). React Native supports this through `accessibilityActions`, Android-only live regions, and `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"` to hide content under sheets ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/docs/accessibility)). Incoming messages should be announced politely only while the person is in that thread, never by stealing focus. Since Android 14, text can scale to **200% on a nonlinear curve**, and Google says to test at maximum size and avoid fixed text heights ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/14/features)); `maxFontSizeMultiplier` belongs only on cramped chrome such as tab labels, never on message text ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/docs/text)). `AccessibilityInfo.isReduceMotionEnabled()` also reports true when Android's animation scale is off ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/docs/accessibilityinfo)), so Realm's existing "Pause motion" setting and the system setting should both collapse transitions to instant or opacity-only changes. Several WCAG 2.2 AA criteria bite chat apps specifically: slide-to-lock or slide-to-cancel voice notes need a tap alternative (2.5.7), "Sending" and "Failed" states need status announcements (4.1.3), sign-in must allow paste and password managers (3.3.8), and auto-dismissing snackbars should respect the user's "time to take action" setting (2.2.1). The W3C's mobile mapping of WCAG 2.2 is still only a draft note ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2mobile-22/)). The European Accessibility Act has applied since 28 June 2025 ([Covington](https://www.insideglobaltech.com/2025/06/10/european-accessibility-act-june-2025-deadline-has-arrived/)), and its harmonised standard EN 301 549 adds native-app requirements beyond WCAG ([Acquia](https://www.acquia.com/blog/european-accessibility-act-and-en-301-549-your-complete-compliance-guide)). Whether a non-telecom messaging app falls in scope is unsettled in the sources, so WCAG 2.2 AA plus EN 301 549 is the safe engineering target.

Play policy turns several safety features into launch requirements. Apps that create accounts must offer deletion both in-app and through a web link ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)). User-generated-content apps need terms acceptance, in-app reporting of users and content, ongoing moderation, and **blocking for any one-to-one contact** ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876937)). Social apps must self-certify Child Safety Standards: published standards against child sexual exploitation, a reporting mechanism, and a named contact ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14747720)). Broad media permissions are reserved for apps the system photo picker cannot serve ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180)), native binaries must support 16 KB pages ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/blog/2025/08/12/react-native-0.81)), and the Play Age Signals API already applies in Brazil and Texas, with more jurisdictions announced ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/google/play/age-signals/use-age-signals-api)). Realm's web safety module (reports with auto-hide, block and mute, Space moderator tools) needs reachable entry points on every message, profile, DM header and Space in the app, which also serves the owner's rule that moderation must not depend on manual review.

## Expo Go cannot carry Realm past this month

Realm's app runs on SDK 57 with React Native 0.86.3. SDK 57 shipped on 30 June 2026 with Reanimated 4.5, react-native-worklets 0.10 and Gesture Handler 2.32 ([Expo SDK 57](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-57)). It inherited a Hermes V1 memory regression affecting apps that use Reanimated or worklets, and the changelog contradicts itself on the fix, citing `expo@57.0.9` in one place and `expo@57.0.17` (React Native 0.86.3) in an August update. Realm's 0.86.3 pin suggests it is already on the fixed line, but the `expo` package should be confirmed at 57.0.17 or later. Then the clock: **SDK 58 was announced on 15 October 2026 with React Native 0.88, and the store build of Expo Go will support only SDK 58 within a few weeks** ([Expo SDK 58](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58)). An SDK 57 Expo Go can still be installed from expo.dev/go or through the CLI ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/troubleshooting/expo-go-version-mismatch/)), but that is a stopgap, and every core Realm feature beyond text chat needs native code anyway.

| Capability | Status in Expo Go | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Remote push via FCM | Unavailable on Android since SDK 53; local notifications still work | [Expo notifications](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/notifications/) |
| LiveKit voice rooms | Not compatible; native WebRTC requires a development build | [LiveKit](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/sdk-platforms/expo/) |
| MMKV v4 key-value storage | A Nitro module that needs prebuild | [react-native-mmkv](https://github.com/mrousavy/react-native-mmkv) |
| Encrypted SQLite (SQLCipher) | Not supported | [expo-sqlite](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/sqlite/) |
| MessagingStyle notifications via react-native-notify-kit | Config plugin, development builds only | [notify-kit](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fmarcocrupi%2Freact-native-notify-kit) |
| Voice foreground service | Needs manifest permissions and a service declaration | [LiveKit RN](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-react-native) |
| Keyboard controller, expo-sqlite, expo-image, expo-audio, expo-video, FlashList v2, Legend List | Work in Expo Go (lists are JavaScript-only) | [Expo bundled modules](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-57/packages/expo/bundledNativeModules.json) |

The path from Windows is short. Add `expo-dev-client`, define an EAS development profile that outputs an **APK** (app bundles cannot be sideloaded) ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/build-reference/apk/)), build in the cloud, and install with Expo Orbit over USB or from a QR code, overriding Android's sideload warning ([Expo tutorial](https://docs.expo.dev/tutorial/eas/android-development-build)). JavaScript iteration continues with the same `npx expo start` loop; a rebuild is needed only when native dependencies or config plugins change, and after every SDK upgrade ([Expo SDK 57](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-57)). Local `eas build --local` is reported not to run on native Windows, only under WSL ([Expo forums](https://forums.expo.dev/t/android-builds-are-supported-only-on-linux-and-macos/66406)), and a debug build launched from a notification can show a broken splash screen, so notification taps should be tested in a preview build ([Expo notifications](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/notifications/)). The sensible sequence is to move to a development build on SDK 57 now and treat SDK 58 as its own task once its first patches land, since SDK 57 itself needed weeks of patches. SDK 58 flips foreground notifications to show by default, makes `File.write()` asynchronous and drops libSQL from expo-sqlite ([Expo SDK 58](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58)).

### The message list is the hardest component, so hide it behind an interface

Both modern lists can anchor a chat to the bottom without inverting. FlashList v2 is a JavaScript-only rewrite that uses the New Architecture's synchronous layout and keeps the visible content position by default ([Shopify Engineering](https://shopify.engineering/flashlist-v2)); its chat setup uses `startRenderingFromBottom` and `onStartReached` ([FlashList docs](https://shopify.github.io/flash-list/docs/usage)). But **Expo pins FlashList 2.0.2** ([Expo bundled modules](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-57/packages/expo/bundledNativeModules.json)), while the prepend-jitter and large-item fixes arrived in 2.2.3 to 2.3.3 ([FlashList releases](https://github.com/Shopify/flash-list/releases)), and chat issues such as flicker with bottom-start rendering remain open ([FlashList issues](https://github.com/Shopify/flash-list/issues?q=is%3Aissue+chat+maintainVisibleContentPosition)). Legend List v3 is pure TypeScript, offers `alignItemsAtEnd`, `maintainScrollAtEnd` and `initialScrollAtEnd`, advises against inverted lists for chat, and ships `KeyboardAwareLegendList` for chat screens with a floating composer ([Legend List](https://legendapp.com/open-source/list/v3/llms-full.txt)); it too has a dense queue of open chat bugs, including a 1 to 2 pixel Android shift on every prepend ([Legend List issues](https://github.com/LegendApp/legend-list/issues?q=is%3Aissue+chat)). Discord's experience is the warning: it moved its channel list to FlashList v1, saw blanking on low-end Android, went back to its in-house list, which never blanked even on a $50 Galaxy A03s, and built its chat list natively ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/supercharging-discord-mobile-our-journey-to-a-faster-app)). Realm should wrap the thread in its own `MessageList` component, start with Legend List v3 non-inverted with `KeyboardAwareLegendList`, and keep FlashList for feeds, member lists and media grids. Stable keys (the server id, falling back to the client nonce), media dimensions reserved from server metadata, and whole-page prepends prevent most jumps. A non-inverted list also avoids the jumbled TalkBack reading order that inverted lists risk.

The keyboard layer is settled. `react-native-keyboard-controller` 1.21.9 is included in Expo Go on SDK 57 ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/keyboard-controller/)); an older Expo guide says it needs a development build ([Expo](https://docs.expo.dev/guides/keyboard-handling/)), but the SDK-specific reference is the more authoritative source. Its `KeyboardChatScrollView` keeps the layout fixed and moves content, choosing when the keyboard lifts messages, taking a shared value for a growing multiline composer, and freezing while an emoji panel replaces the keyboard ([keyboard-controller docs](https://kirillzyusko.github.io/react-native-keyboard-controller/docs/api/components/keyboard-chat-scroll-view)). Its known Android desync on the New Architecture needs Reanimated 4.3 or later, which SDK 57's 4.5.1 satisfies. The composer should sit in a `KeyboardStickyView`, and the built-in `KeyboardAvoidingView` should stay out of chat.

### The data layer decides whether chat feels instant on a cheap phone

expo-sqlite runs in Expo Go with change listeners, WAL journaling and full-text search ([expo-sqlite](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/sqlite/)), and Drizzle's `useLiveQuery` re-renders components when the data under them changes ([Drizzle](https://orm.drizzle.team/docs/connect-expo-sqlite)). That makes **SQLite the source of truth for Spaces, rooms, messages and the outbox**: the gateway and REST pagination write into it, and screens read from it, which avoids re-render storms from large in-memory arrays. TanStack Query, the most-used and best-liked React data library in State of React 2025 (a web survey) ([certificates.dev](https://certificates.dev/blog/breaking-down-state-of-react-2025-results)), suits REST resources such as profiles and settings, wired to NetInfo and AppState and persisted between launches ([TanStack](https://tanstack.com/query/latest/docs/framework/react/react-native); [TanStack](https://tanstack.com/query/latest/docs/framework/react/plugins/persistQueryClient)). Zustand suits ephemeral state such as connection status, typing users and voice participants, and MMKV can hold drafts and gateway sequence numbers once the development build exists. Optimistic send follows standard practice rather than a published Discord write-up: generate a client nonce, insert a pending row, render it immediately, post with the nonce, reconcile when the gateway echoes it, retry the outbox in order on reconnect, and show tap-to-retry after repeated failure. That requires the `/v1` message endpoint to echo and deduplicate nonces, which should be checked against Realm's backend.

The gateway client belongs in one module outside React, written as a small state machine (connecting, identifying, ready, resuming, backoff). It should send app-level heartbeats and treat two missed acknowledgements as a dead socket, resume with the stored session and last sequence number, fall back to a REST catch-up, use capped exponential backoff with jitter, and skip the backoff the moment NetInfo reports a connection or the app returns to the foreground. Supabase's realtime guidance makes the same foreground-reconnect point ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/realtime-heartbeat-messages)). After a short grace period in the background, the socket should close and FCM should take over. Chat pushes should be high priority, and each one must produce a visible notification, because FCM deprioritises high-priority messages that do not ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/cloud-messaging/android/message-priority)). Typing and presence bursts should be coalesced before they reach the UI.

### Push, voice and motion each need one native decision

For push, `getDevicePushTokenAsync()` returns the raw FCM token, so Realm's existing FCM HTTP v1 sender can deliver directly without Expo's push service. Channels must be created before asking permission, because the Android 13 prompt appears only once a channel exists and a channel's importance cannot change after creation ([Expo notifications](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/notifications/)). Sensible channels are direct messages, mentions and replies, Space messages, voice, social activity, and account security. expo-notifications supports reply actions with a text field, but its docs do not say these render as Android's native inline reply or work when the app has been killed, and they do not document MessagingStyle at all. Notifee was reportedly archived in April 2026, and its fork react-native-notify-kit claims MessagingStyle support, but both claims rest on secondary sources ([notify-kit](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fmarcocrupi%2Freact-native-notify-kit)). Because conversation notifications are the Android baseline, the lowest-risk route is a small Expo Module in Kotlin that posts MessagingStyle notifications with shortcuts and direct reply, after a device test of what expo-notifications already does. Replies from a notification should post over REST with a nonce, not over the socket.

For voice, LiveKit needs its React Native SDK, WebRTC package, Expo plugin and the WebRTC config plugin, plus `registerGlobals()` and an audio session started before connecting ([LiveKit](https://docs.livekit.io/transport/sdk-platforms/expo/)). On Android, the default `communication` audio type gives echo cancellation, `preferredOutputList` controls speaker, earpiece and Bluetooth routing, and a microphone-type foreground service keeps a room alive in the background ([LiveKit RN](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-react-native)). ConnectionService and CallKit matter only for ringing one-to-one calls, so they belong in a later phase. LiveKit's 3.0.0 renamed its WebRTC package without affecting JavaScript-only apps ([LiveKit releases](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-react-native/releases)), but the Expo plugin states no supported SDK range, and duplicate package instances have broken its React context before, so peer versions and deduplication in the pnpm monorepo need checking before install.

For motion, Reanimated 4's CSS animations are recommended for state-driven changes, with worklets kept for gestures and scroll ([Software Mansion](https://swmansion.com/blog/reanimated-4-stable-release-the-future-of-react-native-animations-ba68210c3713)). Margelo's work with Discord found that Reanimated on the New Architecture had been re-cloning every registered animated node each frame; fixing it cut Discord's Android jank by **26%**, and the fix is on by default since Reanimated 4.3 ([Margelo](https://margelo.com/blog/margelo-discord-react-native-performance)). Animated nodes per chat row should still be kept to a minimum. Gesture Handler 3 is stable upstream but not bundled with SDK 57 ([Gesture Handler](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-gesture-handler/docs/guides/upgrading-to-3/)), so swipe-to-reply should be a Gesture Handler 2 pan gesture that yields to vertical scrolling, with one haptic at the threshold. Long-press can open either Expo UI's native Jetpack Compose context menu ([Expo UI](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/ui/jetpack-compose/contextmenu/)) or a custom overlay with a reaction row; the custom overlay suits Realm's brand better, provided it exposes the same actions to TalkBack. On Android, `performAndroidHapticsAsync` replaces the Vibrator-based `impactAsync`, which Expo's docs do not recommend there ([expo-haptics](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/haptics/)). For media, expo-image's ThumbHash placeholders, `recyclingKey`, 16-bit `rgb` decoding and new cache seeding (which lets an optimistic local image take over the final cache key) cover chat images ([expo-image](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/image/)). expo-audio records AAC voice notes with metering and plays at up to 2x ([expo-audio](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/audio/)). expo-file-system's upload task can PUT a raw file to a presigned R2 URL with progress events, provided the `Content-Type` matches what was signed and the code checks the status, because the promise resolves even on errors ([expo-file-system](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/filesystem/)). Discord reached 60 fps on a Galaxy A14 by switching animated emoji from GIF to animated WebP ([Discord](https://discord.com/blog/supercharging-discord-mobile-our-journey-to-a-faster-app)). Profiling should use Android Studio's System Trace exported to Perfetto on a release-like build ([React Native](https://reactnative.dev/docs/profiling)), on a low-end phone as well as the owner's own.

## Build in three waves, and refuse seven traps

The order follows dependencies and differentiation. Messaging is the daily loop, and it forces the foundations everything else needs: the development build, gateway, cache, push and navigation. Voice and presence are the differentiators, and they become cheap once the development build exists. Identity and group tools compound only once friends are present. Running alongside the waves is the owner's standing decision that MLS end-to-end-encrypted DMs land before public launch; until then the app should keep the web's honest "Not end-to-end encrypted yet" chip, and afterwards encryption should be a quiet default explained once. RCS's lock badge shows that mixing encrypted and unencrypted chats forces people to watch for an indicator, which is error-prone ([SC World](https://www.scworld.com/brief/apple-and-google-roll-out-end-to-end-encrypted-rcs-messaging)).

| Wave | What to build | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| First: foundation and the messaging loop | EAS development build; dark config with `expo-system-ui`, insets and back rules; the five-tab shell; gateway client, SQLite store and outbox; Messages inbox (chips, pins, mute, search, requests) and a full-baseline chat screen (Seen, typing, swipe reply, reactions, three pins, edit and delete windows, images with upload progress); Spaces with text rooms; FCM push with channels, in-context permission and reply; invite links, codes and QR; report and block everywhere, in-app account deletion, terms; TalkBack labels and actions and 200% text from the start | A friend can be invited, join a Space and hold a reliable conversation on a mid-range Android phone with the app backgrounded and reopened |
| Second: the live layer | LiveKit voice rooms with one-tap join, themed tiles, persistent call bar, foreground service, output routing and phone-call muting; the Home "here now" strip and presence controls with a "what friends see" preview; the Inbox tab with All, Highlights and Off levels and guarded everyone-pings; quiet "friends are in voice" alerts; MessagingStyle conversation notifications through a Kotlin module; voice notes; the SDK 58 upgrade | Joining a room takes one tap from Home and surviving a screen lock, a Bluetooth switch and an incoming call needs no extra taps |
| Third: identity, friend-group tools, then the feed | Theme colour carried through profile, You tab and call tiles, contrast-checked at pick time; polls and ready checks with end times; events with Going, Maybe and Can't Go; member role tags; squad chips; a mobile quick switcher; hold-to-talk pings; rail and list-detail layouts for foldables; predictive back once the native stack supports it; a feed as its own surface | The app feels unmistakably Realm, and large screens no longer stretch a phone layout |

The traps are as well evidenced as the features. The first is **surfaces people cannot remove**: WhatsApp's Meta AI button is the cautionary tale, so every optional surface (discovery, AI, streaks, read receipts, typing indicators) needs a visible off switch, and the DM list must never carry promotions. The second is **forced or unstable navigation**, Discord's three-redesign pattern; Realm should ship navigation it can keep, give every gesture a button, and never put the Space switcher behind a long-press alone. The third is **presence that leaks**: Instagram's Map and Discord's Invisible complaints argue for granular controls with a preview, an Invisible that really hides, and place pins instead of live location. The fourth is **interruption without consent**: ringing group voice, unguarded everyone-pings and a notification prompt at first launch all spend trust that WhatsApp's notify-not-ring voice chats and admin-gated @all protect. The fifth is **effects over legibility**. Liquid Glass is spreading through WhatsApp and Telegram ([MacMagazine](https://macmagazine.com.br/post/2026/06/23/conversas-do-whatsapp-ganham-nova-interface-com-liquid-glass/)), yet it drew legibility complaints, and Telegram added a switch to turn interface effects off for power saving ([Telegram](https://telegram.org/blog/crafting-android-design-and-more)). Imitating it would make Realm look like everyone's 2026 refresh, against an owner who has already rejected glow-heavy spectacle, and it costs GPU time on Android, so blur should stay out of list rows and surfaces should stay opaque. The sixth is **charging for basic identity or for value people already have**: the Snapchat Memories backlash and Discord's subscriber-only profile themes and call tiles both argue that the theme colour should be free, with paid extras arriving later as genuinely new things. The seventh is **trust claims the product cannot back**: XChat and Bitchat both drew scepticism over their security claims ([TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/111280/x-launches-standalone-xchat-app-on-ios-and-elon-musks-everything-app-dream-looks-further-away-than-ever/index.html); [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/07/08/jack_dorsey_debuts_bitchat/)). Beneath these sit the technical traps the sources flag individually: toasts for undoable actions, "press back again to exit", portrait locks, `adjustResize`, the legacy `SafeAreaView`, inverted chat lists, endless per-row animations, Vibrator-based haptics, and testing only on a flagship phone.

## Conclusion

The gap Realm can exploit is about timing as much as features. Discord has publicly promised desktop-style voice joining, per-server identity and a lower button count on mobile, but every change it ships lands as a forced migration on users with years of muscle memory. Realm has no such debt, so the items on Discord's roadmap are Realm's launch features. The highest-leverage engineering decision is not visual: it is making SQLite the source of truth, with a gateway that lives outside React. That combination decides whether chat opens instantly, survives backgrounding and scrolls without blanking on a cheap Android phone, and in a category where the feature list is identical everywhere, that feel is most of the difference.

The second insight is that Realm's two signature commitments, a world lit by each person's colour and accessibility as a first-class value, meet at a single point: contrast-checking the colour when it is picked. Solve that once, in the token pipeline, and personalisation becomes the brand instead of a legibility risk. Several unknowns should be settled by device tests in the first week of the first wave rather than by more reading: whether expo-notifications' reply action renders as native inline reply, whether the `KeyboardChatScrollView` features in Expo's 1.21.9 pin match the docs, whether LiveKit's plugin builds cleanly on SDK 57, and which list library behaves better on a low-end phone with real Realm messages.
